At about two in the morning on 9 March, the driver of the night bus on Route 12 reported that the signs on Bridge Road were wrong. All fourteen of them read Old Dyke Lane, the name the road had before the north bank was filled in in 1981. [[-7: He stopped at the corner of Block 108 to make sure, and his two passengers got down and looked as well. One of them, a cook on his way home from the coffee shop at the interchange, said he had grown up on Old Dyke Lane and that these were the old enamel signs, blue with a white border. The one by the drain had a chip out of its corner, as it always had.]]

The district office sent one officer. By the time he arrived at 06:40 the signs read Bridge Road again. He wrote that the paint was dry and that [[#4: the old name still showed if he held his torch at an angle]]. [[-4: Two households told him their letters that morning were addressed to Old Dyke Lane. The postman says his round book has always listed the road under both names, and that he did not notice anything different.]] [[note 4: Letters are the Postal Board's business. Not our file.]]

The bridge at the end of the road is inspected every Tuesday. [[+4: The inspector on duty that morning measured it as usual. It was two centimetres longer than the week before. His log has the measurement and nothing about the signs.]] [[note 4: Two centimetres is a lot for a bridge. Ask the inspector what he saw.]]

Nobody saw the signs change, either way. [[-4: The district office asks the Records Office to find out who put them up and who took them down.]] [[+7: The cause was recorded as a printing fault.]] [[+4: The sign workshop has no record of printing any signs that month.]] [[+7: See R-0002, Island Signage Standardisation.]]
