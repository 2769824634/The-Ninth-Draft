Every system at the Records Office stores the year in two digits. At midnight on 31 December 1999 they will read 00, and the Office's machines will decide it is 1900. Heuss was given the job of fixing this in January 1998. He was twenty.

His first plan ran to eleven pages: widen the year field to four digits, test it in every district office, switch over on 9 December. [[-3: He asked for two assistants and a room with a lock.]] [[+3: He was given one assistant, part-time, and the room next to the photocopier. The review board sent the plan back with one line: ||"Nothing wrong with the plan. Cannot be approved."|| Plans 02 and 03 came back the same way.]] [[+6: So did 04, 05 and 06. Plan 06 brought the switch-over forward to 1 October, to leave more time for testing.]] [[+8: So did 07 and 08. Plan 08 leaves no time for testing at all. It switches over at 23:59 on 31 December.]] [[note 3-6: Ask them what is wrong with it. They'll say nothing. Then ask why.]]

The district offices are asked to report any record that comes back with the wrong year. [[+3: Most report none. Hougang reports that some of its records are an hour late, which is a different problem (see R-0003).]] [[+8: In August Woodlands showed what happens without the fix: for eleven minutes its computers lived in 1900 (see E-0003). The Office says all records written during the outage have since been corrected.]]

The deadline is midnight on 31 December 1999, and it cannot be moved. [[+8: Plan 09 is written. Heuss keeps it on his desk and has not handed it in. When asked, he says he is still revising it. ||The last page of plan 09 is a list of names, one for each district.||]]
